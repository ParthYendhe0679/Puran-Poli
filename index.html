Group Event Planning Platform

Suggest ideas, vote, pay your share in advance and confirm the plan in one place.

A website where a group of friends suggests event ideas, votes on them, chooses a common time and splits the costs. Members join a plan by paying their share in advance. The payment is held, not charged. The plan is confirmed automatically when a minimum number of members have paid. 1Project summary

1.1 What we are building

We are building a website that helps a group of friends plan an event together. The website covers the full process, from the first idea to the final payment.

A group uses the website in this order:

  1. Suggest ideas. Any member can add an event idea, for example "Bowling at Andheri, ₹600 per person". Members can also upload their exported WhatsApp group chat, and the website will find the ideas that were already discussed in the chat.
  2. Choose a time. Each member marks the hours when they are free. The website shows the time slot when the most members are free.
  3. Vote. Each member votes on every idea. Voting closes at a fixed deadline, and the website selects one winning idea.
  4. Pay the share in advance. Each member who wants to join pays their share. The payment is only held, not charged.
  5. Confirm the plan. When a minimum number of members have paid, the plan is confirmed automatically. If the minimum is not reached by the deadline, every held payment is released and returned automatically.
  6. Split the final bill. After the event, one member scans the restaurant bill. Each member selects the items they had, and the website calculates how much each person owes and to whom.

Members join by opening a link shared in their group chat and typing their name. They do not need to install an app or create a password.

1.2 Our main difference (USP)

A plan is confirmed only after a minimum number of members have paid their share in advance.

In a group chat, saying "I am coming" has no cost, so many people agree and later drop out. On our website, a member joins a plan by paying their share. The money is held, not charged, until the plan is confirmed. If enough members pay before the deadline, the plan is confirmed and the payments are completed. If not enough members pay, every payment is released automatically, so nobody loses money. This reduces last-minute dropouts, and no single member has to collect money from everyone else.

1.3 What we will show the judges

  1. WhatsApp chat import: we upload a real exported group chat of about 200 messages. The website lists the event ideas that were discussed in it, with the name of the person who suggested each idea.
  2. Plan confirmation by payment: members pay their share using Razorpay test mode. When the last required member pays, the plan status changes to "Confirmed" on every member's screen at the same time.
  3. Voice discussion room: two members discuss the plan by voice for one minute. The website converts the speech to text and adds the ideas, votes and time preferences to the plan automatically.

2.3 The question behind the problem statement

The problem statement asks for four actions: propose, vote, split costs and finalize. Many apps already provide these actions separately. For a strong solution, we must also answer a deeper question:

Why do group plans often fail even when most members want to go?

Section 4 answers this question. Each of our main features is designed to fix one of the reasons.

3Facts and research that support the problem

3.1 Group chats are difficult to manage

  • In a survey of 1,005 Americans, 42% said managing group chats feels like a part-time job, 66% said they feel overwhelmed by group chats, 75% have muted a group chat, and 55% have left a group chat. On average, people spend about 26 minutes a day reading and replying to group messages. (Secure Data Recovery survey)
  • Researchers note that in large groups, planning inside a chat often turns into many opinions and side conversations, and people who mute the chat miss the final plan. (The Conversation)

3.2 People cancel plans often

  • In an Ipsos survey of 2,000 Canadians (September 2024), 75% said friends had cancelled plans with them, and 69% said they had cancelled plans with friends themselves. Nearly half of those who cancelled said their reason was made up or only partly true. (Ipsos)

3.3 Shared money creates problems between friends

  • In a Zelle survey (May to June 2026), 76% of Gen Z respondents who paid for a group expense were not fully paid back. 55% said unpaid money created tension or damaged a friendship. 47% said they went into debt covering group expenses. (Rethinking65)
  • In the same survey, 20% said they cancelled plans or ignored chats to avoid conversations about repayment.
  • In a YouTrip survey (September 2026), 1 in 3 respondents had argued with friends over trip expenses, and 21% said they would not travel with the same group again. (Alvinology)

3.4 Early opinions affect group decisions

  • Research on group discussions shows an effect called "anchoring": the opinions shared first have a strong influence on the final decision. In a group chat, the first few people who reply often decide the outcome for everyone. (Hartmann and Rafiee Rad, Anchoring in Deliberations)

3.5 Facts specific to India

  • India has about 535.8 million WhatsApp users, so most group plans in India start in a WhatsApp group. (Hyperleap)
  • From 1 October 2025, NPCI stopped person-to-person UPI "collect requests" to reduce fraud. A person can no longer send a UPI money request to a friend. The person who owes money must start the payment themselves. (The Week)
  • Splitwise's free plan now allows only about 3 expenses per day, and receipt scanning is available only in the paid plan. (Finny)

3.6 Summary of the problem

Group plans often fail because discussion in the chat does not lead to a clear decision, members can agree without any commitment and later drop out, and collecting money from friends is uncomfortable.

The gap we fill: In the apps listed above, deciding and paying are two separate activities. In our website, they are connected: after the vote, members join by paying their share, and the plan is confirmed only when enough members have paid.

Similar models in other industries: This approach is called an "all-or-nothing" or "minimum participation" model. Kickstarter uses it for crowdfunding: a project receives money only if it reaches its target. Groupon started with a similar rule, where a deal was activated only after a minimum number of people bought it. We apply the same rule to group plans among friends, using Indian payment methods.

9Main features (detailed explanation)

Each feature is explained in the same format: what it is, the problem it solves, how it works, an example, and how we build it.

Feature 1: Plan Confirmation by Advance Payment (our USP)

What it is A member joins a plan by paying their share in advance. The payment is held, not charged. The plan is confirmed automatically when a minimum number of members have paid. If the minimum is not reached by the deadline, every held payment is released and the money returns to the members.

The problem it solves In a group chat, members can say "I am coming" without any commitment. Many of them drop out later, and the cost per person increases for those who remain. Also, one member usually pays for everyone and then has to collect money from each person.

How it works

  1. The organiser sets four values:
    • the total cost (for example, a villa for ₹12,000)
    • the minimum number of members needed (for example, 6)
    • the maximum number of seats (for example, 10)
    • the payment deadline (for example, Friday 8 pm)
  2. The website shows the share per person and a progress bar, for example: "4 of 6 required members have paid. 2 more are needed. Deadline: Friday 8 pm."
  3. A member clicks "Join and pay". The payment is authorised (held) but not taken.
  4. When the 6th member pays, the website takes all held payments and marks the plan as "Confirmed". All members are notified at the same time.
  5. If the deadline passes and fewer than 6 members have paid, the plan is marked "Not confirmed" and all held payments are released automatically.
  6. If more members join than the minimum, the cost per person decreases. The website first holds the higher share (total cost divided by the minimum number) and later refunds the difference to every member automatically.

Member status levels Each member has one of three statuses, visible to the group: "Interested", "Joining" and "Paid". This shows clearly who has actually committed.

Example Eight friends plan a villa stay costing ₹12,000. The organiser sets a minimum of 6 members. The share is ₹2,000 per person (₹12,000 ÷ 6). By Thursday, 7 members have paid, so the plan is confirmed. The final share is ₹1,714 per person (₹12,000 ÷ 7), so each of the 7 members automatically receives ₹286 back.

How we build it

  • In the hackathon (free): Razorpay test mode with "manual capture". In Razorpay, a payment can stay in the "authorised" state (held) until the merchant "captures" it (takes it). If it is not captured within the allowed time, Razorpay refunds it automatically. The maximum hold time is 5 days. (Razorpay documentation)
  • In the real product: UPI Reserve Pay, a UPI feature approved by RBI and operated by NPCI. It blocks an amount of up to ₹10,000 in the customer's own bank account for up to 90 days. The money is debited only when the plan is confirmed. If the plan is not confirmed, the block is removed. (Cashfree documentation)
  • A background job in our Go server checks deadlines and confirms or cancels plans automatically.

Why this is useful The organiser does not have to collect money, members who are not serious do not block the plan, and nobody loses money if the plan does not happen.

Feature 2: WhatsApp Chat Import

What it is A member uploads the exported WhatsApp group chat (a .txt file). The website reads the messages and creates idea cards for the plans that were discussed in the chat.

The problem it solves Most group plans start in a WhatsApp group. After many messages, it is difficult to see which ideas were suggested, who supported them and what is still undecided. Members usually have to scroll up and read everything again.

How it works

  1. The member exports the chat from WhatsApp (Group → More → Export chat → Without media) and uploads the file.
  2. Our Go server reads the file and separates each message into date, time, sender and text. It supports the formats used by both Android and iPhone.
  3. The server removes messages that are not useful, for example "ok", "haha", stickers, <Media omitted>, deleted messages and system messages such as "Rohan joined".
  4. The remaining messages are sent to the Groq AI model, which returns:
    • each event idea and the person who suggested it
    • the members who supported or rejected each idea
    • dates and times mentioned by members
    • members who already said they will join
    • the main point that is still undecided
  5. The website creates the plan with these ideas already added as idea cards.

Example A group chat has 212 messages about a weekend plan. After the upload, the website shows four idea cards: "Bowling at Andheri (suggested by Rohan, supported by 4 members)", "Turf football", "Movie at PVR" and "Lonavala day trip". It also shows a note: "Most members prefer bowling. Saturday or Sunday is not decided yet."

Privacy The chat file is processed once and then deleted. Only the idea cards are saved. If a member mentioned their budget in the chat (for example, "I cannot spend more than ₹500"), that number is not shown to the group. It is shown only to that member as a suggestion for their private budget (Feature 3).

How we build it A parser written in Go for the chat format, and the Groq model openai/gpt-oss-20b with strict JSON mode, which makes the AI return data in an exact format that our code can read.

Feature 3: Private Budget Limits

What it is Each member privately enters the maximum amount they can spend and any conditions they have. Other members never see these values. The website uses them to show which ideas suit everyone.

The problem it solves Some members cannot afford an expensive idea but do not want to say so in front of the group. They either stay silent and pay more than they want, or they drop out.

How it works

  1. Each member enters:
    • their maximum spend per person (for example, ₹800)
    • their conditions, for example "vegetarian food only", "no alcohol", "must be home by 10 pm" or "not more than 45 minutes from Andheri"
  2. The server stores these values separately. There is no API that returns another member's budget, so not even the organiser can see it.
  3. On each idea card, the website shows only one of two labels:
    • "Within everyone's budget"
    • "Above the budget of 2 members" (the number of members, never their names)
  4. The group's comfortable budget range is shown only when at least 3 members have entered their budget, so no individual can be identified.

Example Six members enter their budgets. One member enters ₹500. The "Go-karting, ₹1,200 per person" idea shows "Above the budget of 1 member". The group sees this before voting and can choose another idea, and nobody knows who entered ₹500.

Feature 4: Fair Voting

What it is A voting system designed to give every member an equal say and to always end with one clear result.

The problem it solves In a group chat poll, members can see the results before voting, so the first few votes influence everyone (the anchoring effect described in Section 3.4). Polls have no deadline, and ties are not resolved.

How it works

  1. Three vote options for each idea: "Love it" (2 points), "Okay" (1 point) and "No" (0 points).
  2. Results are hidden until the member votes. A member sees the results only after submitting their own vote.
  3. One veto per member. Each member can mark one idea as "I will not join if this is chosen". That idea is removed. Because each member has only one veto per plan, it is used only for serious reasons.
  4. Budget fit: an idea that is above the budget of some members receives a lower score (Section 13.2 shows the formula).
  5. Turn-based fairness: the website remembers previous plans of the same group. If a member's first choice lost in the last two plans, their "Love it" vote counts 1.5 times in the next plan. The website shows this openly, for example: "Riya's first choice was not selected in the last two plans, so her vote has extra weight this time."
  6. Deadline: voting closes at the set time.
  7. Tie-breaking: if the top two ideas are within 5% of each other, the Groq AI selects one and gives a short reason, for example: "Bowling is selected because it is within everyone's budget and is closest for 5 of the 6 members." The group can also choose a random selection instead.

Example Bowling receives 9 points and turf football receives 9 points. Bowling is within everyone's budget, but turf football is above the budget of one member. The tie-breaker selects bowling and shows the reason.

Feature 5: Availability Heatmap

What it is A weekly grid where each member marks the hours when they are free. The website shows the time slot when the most members are available.

The problem it solves Finding a common time through chat messages takes many replies, and members often miss the final decision.

How it works

  1. Each member taps or drags over the hours when they are free. The grid has large cells, so it is easy to use on a phone.
  2. A cell becomes darker when more members are free at that time.
  3. The website highlights the best time slot, for example: "Saturday 6 pm to 9 pm: 7 of 8 members are free."
  4. The organiser can mark some members as "required", for example the person whose birthday it is. The best slot will always include the required members.
  5. Optional: members can import their busy times from Google Calendar (free API) instead of marking them manually.

Feature 6: Receipt Scanner with Item-wise Split

What it is One member takes a photo of the restaurant bill. The website reads all items from the photo. Each member then selects the items they had on their own phone, and the website calculates each person's exact share.

The problem it solves Splitting a bill equally is unfair when members order different items. Calculating each person's share by hand, including GST and service charge, is slow and leads to mistakes.

How it works

  1. Scan: a member uploads a photo of the bill. The Groq vision model qwen/qwen3.8-27b reads each line: item name, quantity, price, GST, service charge, discount and total.
  2. Check: our Go code adds the item prices and compares the result with the subtotal on the bill. If the numbers do not match, the website highlights the line so a member can correct it.
  3. Select items: every member opens the bill on their own phone (through the plan link or a QR code) and selects the items they had. The selections appear on all phones immediately. If several members select the same item, its cost is divided between them.
  4. Calculate:
    • GST and service charge are divided in proportion to each member's food total.
    • Alcoholic drinks are divided only between the members who selected them.
    • The website also shows that, under CCPA guidelines (2022), service charge on restaurant bills in India is voluntary, so customers may ask for it to be removed.
  5. Settle: the website calculates the minimum number of payments needed (Section 13.5). For example, 6 members with 3 different payers can settle with 3 payments instead of 9.
  6. Pay: each member sees a button such as "Pay ₹347 to Aman". The button opens a UPI app (Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm) with the amount and note already filled in. On a laptop, a QR code is shown instead.
  7. Reminders: the website sends polite reminders to members who have not paid, so the person who paid does not have to ask.

Example A bill of ₹2,360 includes 2 pizzas, 4 drinks, GST and service charge. Aman paid the bill. Each member selects their items. The website shows: Riya owes Aman ₹412, Rohan owes Aman ₹389, and so on, each with a UPI payment button.

Backup If the AI service is not available, the bill is read with Tesseract.js, a free text-recognition library that runs in the browser.

Feature 7: Voice Discussion Room with Automatic Notes

What it is A live voice call for the group with a 5-minute timer. While members talk, the website converts their speech to text and adds the ideas, votes and time preferences to the plan automatically.

The problem it solves Some decisions are faster to discuss by voice than by typing many messages, but decisions made on a call are usually not written down, and members who were not on the call do not know what was decided.

How it works

  1. A member starts the voice room. Other members join from the plan link. Recording starts only after every member agrees.
  2. Each member's browser records only that member's microphone, in 15-second parts, and only while the member is speaking. Because each recording comes from one member's phone, the website always knows who said what.
  3. The audio is sent to our Go server, which uses Groq whisper-large-v3-turbo to convert it to text. Whisper also understands sentences that mix Hindi and English.
  4. Every 20 seconds, the new text is sent to the Groq model openai/gpt-oss-20b, which returns new ideas, votes, preferred times, budget mentions and open questions.
  5. The plan page updates on every member's screen.
  6. After 5 minutes, the website shows a summary, for example: "Decided: Bowling, Saturday 6 pm, about ₹600 per person. Confirm this decision?" A member confirms it with one click.
  7. The audio is deleted after it is converted to text.

Example Rohan says, "Let's do bowling at Andheri, but nothing too expensive." Riya says, "Bowling is fine, but only after 6 pm." The plan shows a new idea card "Bowling, Andheri", a "Love it" vote from Rohan, an "Okay" vote from Riya, and the note "Riya: after 6 pm".

Use in person The same feature works when the group is sitting together. One phone is placed on the table and records the discussion.

Feature 8: Event Day Page and Event Summary Video

Event Day Page

  • Schedule: the day's plan in time order, for example "6:00 pm meet at Andheri station, 6:30 pm bowling, 8:30 pm dinner at Lokhandwala".
  • Who is on the way: a member can share their location for one hour. Other members see the estimated arrival time, for example "Aman: 12 minutes away". A member can also click "I will be late" and the schedule updates for everyone.
  • Shared photos: all members add photos and short videos (up to 10 seconds) to one shared page.
  • Live video for members who could not attend: one member can start a live video so that members who could not attend can watch an important moment, for example a cake cutting. Only members of the plan can watch.

Event Summary Video

  • After the event, the website creates a 30-second vertical video in the browser.

Feature 9: Fair Meeting Location

What it is The website suggests a meeting place where the longest travel time for any member is as short as possible.

The problem it solves When a group chooses a central place by guessing, one member may travel 90 minutes while others travel 10 minutes.

How it works

  1. Each member enters their starting point or selects their nearest railway station.
  2. The website finds suitable places (for example, bowling alleys or cafes) around the middle of all starting points, using OpenStreetMap data.
  3. It calculates the travel time from each member to each place.
  4. It selects the place where the longest travel time is the shortest, and shows each member's travel time.
  5. Mumbai local train support: for Mumbai, the website uses a simple table of travel times between stations on the Western, Central and Harbour lines, because train travel times are very different from road distances.

Example Members start from Borivali, Andheri, Dadar and Bandra. The website suggests a place near Andheri, where every member reaches within 35 minutes.

13How the technical parts work

13.1 WhatsApp Chat Import process

  1. Read the file (Go code, no AI). Each line such as 12/10/26, 9:41 pm - Rohan: bowling chalein? is split into date, time, sender and text. Both Android and iPhone formats are supported. A line without a date is added to the previous message, because it is part of a multi-line message.
  2. Remove unnecessary messages (Go code, no AI). System messages ("joined", "left"), <Media omitted>, "This message was deleted", and very short messages such as "ok", "haha" or "?" are removed.
  3. Select relevant messages. Only the last 7 days, or the last 400 messages after cleaning, are kept. Messages with planning words are given priority (place names, numbers, days of the week, and words such as "plan", "book", "let's", "chalo", "kab", "kahan", "kitna").
  4. Send to Groq (AI). The messages are sent in batches to openai/gpt-oss-20b with strict JSON mode, so the answer always has this exact format:
{
  "ideas": [{"title": "Bowling", "place": "Andheri", "cost_per_person": 600,
             "proposed_by": "Rohan", "liked_by": ["Aman", "Riya"], "disliked_by": []}],
  "times_mentioned": [{"by": "Riya", "text": "Saturday after 6"}],
  "already_joining": ["Rohan", "Aman"],
  "budget_hints": [{"by": "Kunal", "max": 500}],
  "undecided": "Saturday or Sunday"
}
  1. Combine results (Go code). Ideas with the same name from different batches are combined, and their supporters are counted.
  2. Create the plan. Ideas become idea cards. Budget hints are shown only to the member who mentioned them, on their private budget screen.

Free usage limit: each gpt-oss model allows 8,000 tokens per minute on the free plan. 400 short messages are about 6,000 tokens. For larger chats, the work is divided between gpt-oss-20b, gpt-oss-120b and qwen3.8-27b, because each model has a separate limit.

13.2 Voting score formula

For each idea:

points        = (Love it x 2) + (Okay x 1)
                (a "Love it" vote from a member with turn-based priority counts x 1.5)
budget_fit    = members whose budget fits / members who entered a budget
final_score   = points x (0.5 + 0.5 x budget_fit)
vetoed idea   = removed from the vote
tie           = top two scores within 5% → AI tie-breaker or random selection

The website shows this calculation to any member who clicks "Why did this idea win?".

13.3 How private budgets stay private

  • Budgets are stored in a separate database table.
  • No API returns another member's budget value.
  • The server answers only two questions:
    • "Is this idea within everyone's budget?" The answer is "Yes" or "Above the budget of N members".
    • "What is the group's comfortable budget range?" This is answered only if 3 or more members have entered a budget.
  • The organiser and the admin screens also see only these answers.

13.4 Plan statuses (for Feature 1)

DRAFT → VOTING → COLLECTING PAYMENTS → CONFIRMED → EVENT DAY → SETTLING → COMPLETED
                         │
                         └──→ NOT CONFIRMED (deadline passed) → all held payments released
  • Collecting payments: each "Join and pay" creates a Razorpay test-mode payment with manual capture. Its status is "authorised" (held).
  • When the number of paid members reaches the minimum: a Go background job captures all held payments, the plan status changes to "Confirmed", and a live update is sent to all members.
  • If the deadline passes first: a background job (River) changes the status to "Not confirmed". The payments are not captured, so Razorpay refunds them automatically.
  • If more members join after confirmation: the difference is refunded to all members through the Razorpay refund API.
  • Reliability: every step is saved in a log table. If the server restarts, the River jobs continue from where they stopped.

13.5 Settlement in the minimum number of payments

  1. For each member, calculate: balance = amount paid − amount owed.
  2. Make two lists: members who should receive money and members who should pay.
  3. Match the member who owes the most with the member who should receive the most. The payment is the smaller of the two amounts. Remove any member whose balance becomes zero. Repeat.
  4. This needs at most (number of members − 1) payments. For 6 members, that is at most 5 payments, instead of up to 15 if every member paid every other member.

13.6 Bill split calculation

member_food   = sum of (item price ÷ number of members who selected the item)
member_tax    = (member_food ÷ total food) x (GST + service charge − discount)
member_drinks = alcoholic items, divided only between members who selected them
member_total  = member_food + member_drinks + member_tax
                (rounded to whole rupees; any rounding difference is assigned to the payer)

13.7 Voice Discussion Room process

  1. Call: LiveKit (free plan) connects all members' audio.
  2. Recording: each browser records only its own member's microphone, in 15-second parts, and only when the member is speaking (checked by sound level).
  3. Speech to text: each part goes to our Go server and then to Groq whisper-large-v3-turbo. The speaker is known because the audio came from their phone.
  4. Text to plan data: every 20 seconds, Go sends the new text to gpt-oss-20b (strict JSON) and asks for new ideas, votes, times, budget mentions and open questions.
  5. Live update: the result is sent to all members through WebSocket, and the plan page updates.
  6. Summary: after 5 minutes, the website shows the decisions and asks for confirmation.

Free usage limit: Whisper allows 20 requests per minute. Four members sending one part every 15 seconds make 16 requests per minute, which is within the limit.

13.8 Fair Meeting Location calculation

  1. Find the centre point of all members' starting locations.
  2. Find suitable places near the centre using the OpenStreetMap Overpass API (free), filtered by the type of idea (bowling, cafe, turf).
  3. Calculate the travel time from each member to each place using OSRM (free) or the Mumbai local train table.
  4. Select the place where the longest travel time is the smallest.

13.9 Event Summary Video

  • Created in the browser using an HTML canvas and the browser's built-in video recorder (MediaRecorder).
  • Each scene is a simple animated card: the number of ideas, the winning vote, the confirmation, five photos and the cost summary.
  • No server is needed, and the video is ready in about 10 seconds.

14AI plan (Groq, free tier)

14.1 Our rule for using AI

Our code makes the decisions. AI reads and writes text. Members approve important actions.

  • Money calculations, deadlines and plan confirmation are always done by our Go code, so the results are exact.
  • AI is used only to read unstructured input (chats, bill photos, speech) and to write short explanations.
  • Important actions (confirming a plan, paying, removing an idea) always need a member's click.

  • Tasks that do not need AI are done in code: cleaning chats, calculations, deadlines and scores.

  • Large tasks are divided between three models, because each model has its own limit.
  • Demo data is prepared once and saved before the hackathon.
  • If the AI service is not available, each feature has a backup:
    • Chat import uses keyword matching.
    • The receipt scanner uses Tesseract.js.
    • The voice room lets members type notes instead.

  • Our Go code has one function for structured decisions. By default it uses Groq gpt-oss-20b with strict JSON, which is free. If we receive free Jev credits, we can switch this function to Jev by changing one setting.
  • Suitable tasks for Jev are classifying each chat message (idea, vote, time or not relevant) and classifying each bill line (food, drink, alcohol, tax or discount).

23UI and UX design

23.1 Visual style

  • Overall look: bright and friendly, with large rounded cards and clear outlines. The design should feel suitable for friends planning an outing, not for a bank.
  • Colours: one main colour (violet) for primary actions and confirmation, green for "within budget" and "paid", orange for "above budget" and "waiting", and a light background. Dark mode is also supported.
  • Fonts: a bold, rounded font for headings (for example, Unbounded) and a clear font for body text (for example, Poppins).
  • Mobile first: most members will open the link from WhatsApp on a phone. Buttons are at least 44 pixels high, each screen has one main action, and the main navigation is at the bottom of the screen.

23.2 Important interactions

  • Plan confirmation: the progress bar fills, the phone vibrates, and the plan status changes to "Confirmed".
  • Lower share: when a new member pays, the share per member updates with a short animation.
  • Bill selection: small profile circles appear on each item as members select it.
  • Chat import: the website shows progress while it reads the messages, then displays the ideas it found.

23.3 Design rules

  • Each screen clearly shows what the member needs to do next.
  • All deadlines are always visible.
  • Budget values are never shown to other members. Reminders are polite, and the website does not publicly highlight members who have not paid.
  • Pages load quickly on slow internet: small page size and no large images on the first load.
  • Accessibility: good colour contrast, readable text sizes, support for screen readers, and information is never shown by colour alone.

27Pitch guidelines and judge questions

27.1 Advice from our senior (previous winner)

  1. Build a good user interface.
  2. Have a clear USP (ours: a plan is confirmed only when a minimum number of members pay in advance).
  3. Make the main part reusable as an npm or Go package or SDK (ours: fairsplit, chat2plan, <group-poll> and the MCP server).
  4. Use AI in a meaningful way (ours: WhatsApp Chat Import, bill reading and the Voice Discussion Room).
  5. Be confident and answer positively. Do not use negative sentences.

27.2 How we answer questions

Phrases we use:

  • "Good question. We handle that with…"
  • "Yes, and in addition…"
  • "That is our next step, and our design already supports it because…"

Phrases we avoid:

  • "We did not implement that."
  • "It does not work yet."
  • "We did not have time."
  • "It is only fake data."

27.3 Possible judge questions and answers

"How is this different from WhatsApp polls and Splitwise?" WhatsApp polls record votes, and Splitwise records expenses after the money is spent. Our website connects the two: after the vote, members join by paying their share, and the plan is confirmed only when enough members have paid. This is the step that existing apps do not provide.

"Why would members pay before the plan is confirmed?" The payment is only held, not charged. If the plan is not confirmed, the payment is released automatically. Also, the share per member decreases when more members join, so members have a reason to invite others instead of dropping out.

"What if a member cannot afford an idea?" Each member sets a private budget. Ideas that are above any member's budget are marked before voting, so the group can choose an idea that every member can afford. No member's budget is shown to others.

"Is it safe to upload a WhatsApp chat?" The chat file is processed once and deleted. Only the idea cards are saved. Budget amounts mentioned in the chat are shown only to the member who mentioned them. We also check uploaded text with a safety model before it is sent to the AI.

"Is it allowed to hold money in this way?" Yes. It is a standard payment feature, also used by hotels and cab services. In the demo, we use Razorpay's manual capture in test mode. For the real product, we will use UPI Reserve Pay, which RBI and NPCI introduced to block money in the customer's own account for up to 90 days.

"Why not send UPI requests to members?" Since 1 October 2025, NPCI does not allow person-to-person collect requests, to reduce fraud. Therefore we provide a payment link with the amount already filled in, and the website sends reminders.

"Why did you choose Go?" Our website has many live connections and timers: votes, item selections, deadlines and refunds. Go handles many of these at the same time with low memory use. LiveKit, which we use for voice, is also built in Go.

"What does it cost to run?" Nothing. All services are on free plans: Groq, Neon, Vercel, Render, LiveKit, Cloudinary, OpenStreetMap and Razorpay test mode. UPI payment links do not need a payment gateway.

"What if the AI reads the bill incorrectly?" The AI only reads the bill. Our code checks that the items add up to the bill total and highlights any line that does not match. Each member also selects their own items, so every member checks their part.

"What if the free Groq limit is reached?" We save AI answers, divide work between three models, and every AI feature has a free backup: keyword matching for chats, Tesseract.js for bills and typed notes for the voice room.

"How will this scale?" The Go servers do not keep session data in memory, so we can run several servers. PostgreSQL uses indexes, and background jobs continue after restarts. The same design works for a small number of plans or for many thousands.

"How is this different from Partiful or Howbout?" These apps focus on invitations and calendars. Marking "Going" has no commitment, and they do not handle payments. Our website covers the full process from ideas to payment, and it is designed for India: UPI payments, WhatsApp chat import, support for Hindi and English speech, and no app installation.
